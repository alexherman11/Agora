3 · The idea under it imagined

Every piece of work has one ball, and one person holds it

The ball is "whose move is it". A finished worker's ball is with its owner, to review it. A landed change's ball is with its owner, to check it live. An unowned card's ball is with JEFF, to give it to someone. A design question's ball is with you. Today the ball lives in prose (next_action, a Shop talk line). The governor makes it a fact it can read, and the agents can set it with one word.

The rule the governor keeps: the House may only go quiet when every ball is with Alex or with a running worker.

4 · How it works imagined

The governor, step by step

It is code in the House, not another model. It costs nothing to run, does the same thing every time, and has an off switch. The judgement stays with JEFF and the residents. The governor decides who should be thinking, and what about.

WHEN

A turn ends, or work moves

Any agent's turn ends. A worker finishes a turn, passes review, lands or fails. A card changes owner or lane. A slot frees up. Never on a clock.

LOOK

It reads one slate

Slots in use per provider. Who is busy, idle or down. Every ball: who holds it, what kind, since when. Ready cards with their size.

DECIDE

It applies your rules, in order

The rules below, as a section of the Agent Book you can edit. Each free agent gets at most one nudge, listing at most three balls.

ACT

Nudge, file, or rest

It wakes an agent with its moves, puts your balls in Needs Me, or stays quiet. It never starts workers, restarts anything or changes cards itself.

  1. No ball sits with a free agent.A free agent holding balls is woken with them, oldest first.
  2. Finish before starting.Review, land, check and close come before new cards. Ten finished workers come before a new one.
  3. Fill free slots, small first.A free resident with no finish-balls gets its next ready card. While a large job runs, one Claude and one Codex slot stay open for small cards.
  4. A card that is yours needs no go.JEFF's assignment is the go. A resident who would wait for permission is told to start.
  5. Manager moves go to JEFF.Ready cards with no owner, balls held by an agent that is down, a ball that didn't move after its holder's turn, and your asks that have no card.
  6. Your balls go to Needs Me, never to a chat.The governor never wakes you. JEFF tells you only what needs you.
  7. The same picture never wakes anyone twice.If a nudge changes nothing, that ball goes to JEFF as stalled. If JEFF can't move it either, it goes to you. No loops.
  8. A budget, and an off switch.At most 12 nudges an hour across the House by default, and governor=off on the House settings stops it.

7 · Picking up work while waiting imagined
Small things flow past big ones

  
A resident whose worker is running is free. It doesn't sit and wait. The governor hands it the next thing it can move, so small jobs finish while a long one runs. Three sizes, set when a card is scoped:

  

    
Tiny
Done inside the agent's own turn: close a card, answer an ask, look something up, hand something on. No worker.

    
Small
One worker turn, about half an hour or less. These get the open slot while a large job runs.

    
Large
Several worker turns, or a story. At most one large job per resident at a time, so each resident can keep track.

8 · Other ways I weighed
Why code, why turn ends, why not a timer

  

    
A small model as governorMore flexible on messy cards. But it is a model call on every turn end, another prompt to keep right, and it can talk itself into loops.Not first. Maybe later, only to size and tidy cards once.

    
A heartbeat timerSimple. But it breaks your no-timers rule, spends usage while nothing has changed, and still wouldn't know whose move it is.No.

    
Tell JEFF to keep goingA prompt can't outlast the end of a turn. He also can't see what changed since, and the old header teaches him to hold.Needed anyway (Agent Book), not enough alone.

    
Residents pull, no managerFast for cards that already have an owner. But nobody assigns new work, rebalances, or notices someone is down.Part of it: residents pull, JEFF assigns.
