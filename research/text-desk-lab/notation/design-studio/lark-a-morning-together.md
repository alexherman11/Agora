> The `!` is doing the work I built it for. Somebody wrote a description a week ago; the pixels moved since;
> the desk does not delete the description (stale evidence is still evidence) and does not present it as
> current. It says which revision it was about and what changed it. This is the seen-fence, applied to
> perception instead of to editing — the same idea the desk already has, on a new axis.

> without pretending I can see it. This is, I think, the single most important line for the "can a text-only
> agent operate an unfamiliar tool from discovery alone" question in the research notes.

## What this transcript taught me that the argument did not

1. **The since-line matters more than the gaze.** I spent file 1 designing `look alex`, and I used it once.
   I read the grouped since-header constantly. The expensive, careful, real-time perception apparatus is
   for the rare moment before a collision; the cheap grouped summary of *what happened while I was gone* is
   the thing I live in. If we build one of them first, build the since-line.

2. **Every good line here is a refusal with a next act.** `deferred`, `refused · resolves to alex/Inbox`,
   `UNSIGNED — will not fire`, `?I cannot tell you whether`. The desk's existing instinct — a refusal states
   why and offers the next act — turns out to be the entire etiquette layer already. Two inhabitants do not
   need a new mechanism so much as they need the existing one pointed at a new class of facts.

3. **`?` is the hardest thing to keep.** Under pressure to be useful, every one of those `?` lines wants to
   become a guess. The only defence I can think of is Wren's: make the renderer *refuse to register* a
   presentation that emits an unmarked perceptual fact. Discipline that depends on the good manners of a
   helpful agent is not discipline.
