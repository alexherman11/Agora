      The design

      Four moves on top of what exists

      Each move reuses a word or a field that is already there. Nothing on this list is built yet imagined.

      • 1

        One way to lead workers, for any agent you list

        Rowan's guarded entry becomes the only way an agent leads workers, and it is not Rowan's alone. The list of who may lead, and which cards each leads, stays in a local file only you can change, as Rowan's is. An agent can never widen its own reach. Sable and Pike get a builder session and a line in that file when you add them.

      • 2

        Workers report to their lead, and JEFF hears the lead

        Today a worker's result would wake both Rowan and JEFF: JEFF's task rows still count builder changes on cards that name him. On a card with a lead, builder changes stop waking JEFF. He wakes when the lead writes the card's status, blocker or result. Same rows, one field list changed.

      • 3

        Three kinds of news rise a level

        Done and checked against what was asked. Needs someone above: a permission, a decision, something only you can do. The job changed: it turned out to be a different job. Everything else stays with the lead. This goes into the rules for leads and for JEFF, and the card's resolver field names who it waits on.

      • 4

        You see the whole tree, and can talk to any part of it

        The work view groups workers under their lead, and leads under JEFF. Click a worker to open its chat. The lead is in that chat too, so what you say there reaches it.

      Costs and doubts

      What it costs, and what would change my mind

      Cost. Every lead is a model conversation that wakes on its workers' results. With two leads running two workers each, a busy hour is about 4 worker turns, 4 to 8 short lead turns and a few JEFF turns. Move 2 removes JEFF's duplicate wakes, which is the cheapest saving on the list.

      What would change my mind. If leads only relay, and every worker event still climbs to JEFF in practice, the middle layer is pure cost. After a week of Rowan I'd count two things: lines reaching you per finished card (aim: one or two), and how often a lead caught a problem before JEFF or you did. If the second is near zero, flatten back to JEFF and workers.

      A word on "three". Rowan was built with exactly three cards. I first read your "three conversations" as three agents under JEFF. Question 2 asks which you meant.
