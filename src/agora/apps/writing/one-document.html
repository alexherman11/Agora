Two people at once · redrafted 4 Nov

One shared history, and help where it collides

Each document's text becomes a CRDT: everyone's typing combines the same way on every device, even offline, and every change is kept with who made it. A CRDT can't understand meaning, though. When two people rewrite the same sentence without seeing each other, it keeps both and the words run together. That is the one place Agora's intelligence helps. It offers a fix, and a person decides.

Your question · Automerge 3

How keeping every keystroke got cheap

A CRDT remembers every change: who, where and what. Stored one change after another, that is huge. Automerge stores it in columns instead: all the authors together, all the positions together, all the inserted letters together. Columns repeat themselves, so they shrink. “Alex, Alex, Alex…” becomes “Alex × 900”, and positions that each go up by one become “+1 × 900”. read

Version 2 already saved this way but unpacked it into memory when you opened a document. Version 3 (July 2025) works on the packed form directly. Their example: pasting Moby Dick took 700 MB of memory in version 2 and 1.3 MB in version 3. A document that hadn't opened after 17 hours opened in 9 seconds. read automerge.org

How it fits together · redrafted

Seven pieces for the history

1. The body is a CRDT; the world still decides. The text is saved as packed bytes beside the log, and the log keeps small facts: version, words, who. Your page keeps a copy, so it works with no signal. The kernel merges and passes changes to everyone with the page open. The CRDT says how edits combine; the kernel says whose count (the author decides, your answer 4).

2. Two kinds of writer. People typing live send each keystroke, and see each other's cursors, so collisions stay rare. Whole-text writers (JEFF, imports, today's editor) say which version they read. Sentences changed since are held instead of overwritten. This is Sable's rule from the October lab: 0 silent errors in 50 checks. read POSITION.md §5.2. Still open: Loro text can't record a moved paragraph as a move, so moves need paragraphs stored as a list, or the kernel spotting them.

3. Collisions are marked, never hidden. “Two versions here”, with both originals in the margin. For live typists the CRDT has already merged, so the mark is found afterwards, from overlapping changes inside one sentence. No AI needed to see or fix it.

4. Intelligence offers, people decide. It works after the fact, at a pause or a sentence, never at typing speed. The biggest use: agents cause most collisions, so an agent reworks its own held edit onto the latest text. For people, where intelligence is on, Combine proposes one sentence. Accepting it is an edit signed by the agent, and it can be undone. JEFF's bigger rewrites arrive as a suggestion you merge or discard, as Ink & Switch's bots did on branches.

5. Suggest. Someone who can read but not edit presses Suggest. Their changes wait, marked, for the author to merge or decline. Neighbours and agents use the same path. new idea

6. History you can read. Grouped by person and pause: “Bea, 10:42, added the hearing date”. Rewind, restore a paragraph, see who wrote a sentence. Summaries come from intelligence when it's on; otherwise a plain line, “Bea changed 2 paragraphs”.

7. Comments hold characters. A comment remembers its first and last character, so it follows its words through any edit. ran it survived a concurrent edit from another device. Your double comments become two anchors on the same words. A comment is intact, changed (its words were partly rewritten, so it shows the original quote) or detached (its words are gone). The live-typing path detaches more often than whole-text writes do, but always visibly. Highlights work the same way and are shared by default (your answer 5).

Plus: removal. Keeping everything is bad when someone pastes a phone number or a home address. “Remove from history” cuts the history at a point, destroys the earlier bytes, and says “earlier history cleared by Alex”. The Constitution already gives people the right to have their history deleted.

Still as before: one document in many places (Writing and the board point at the same thing), the continuous editor with the Clean look for neighbours (your answer 3), and $ references of all three sorts (your answer 1).

Beyond documents

What else in Agora needs merging

Today the world keeps every value's history, refuses a stale write instead of merging it, and undoes by putting old values back. read Different things need different rules. The idea: each kind of value says how it combines. Kinds already go down to values, so this is one more fact on a kind, not a new system. It first needs writes recorded as operations (“add this”, “move that”) instead of new values, which the October lab specified and nobody built. imagined

Kind of valueHow two changes combineIn Agora
A plain valueThe last change wins, and the earlier one stays in historya card's lane, a pin's place, an event's start
A setAdds and removes both landvotes, members, tags, who's going
A list or treeMoves stay moves; nothing driftsagenda items, folders, lane order, window layout
ProseCRDT text with one historydocuments, verb steps, help topics, JEFF's notes
A definitionNever merged live: try it on a branch, then switch over to a fixed versionlenses, styles, rules, components

Definitions are where intelligence matters most. Dynamic Desk wants people to follow a shared feature “until they choose to change something”. Today a kit copies a lens and never hears from it again. A kit that keeps following its origin needs a three-way merge of your change against theirs. Explaining what changed upstream, and adapting your version to it, is work for JEFF. That is the Seabright “features spread, people opt out” story.

Worlds and experiments (private worlds, sandboxes, the parallel worldline) merge back the same way: each change by its kind, clean ones applied, conflicts held. The October lab specified this and it was never built.

History, comments and removal should work on anything: the same readable history for a pin, a card or a lens as for a document; comments on any thing; one redact word (the log can already hold a redacted value, and nothing uses it yet).

No merging needed: chat lines (each is its own thing, only its speaker edits it) and screen layout, which stays on your screen.

Why not make the whole world one CRDT? A CRDT syncs whole. Agora shows each thing only to the people allowed to see it. So CRDTs live inside values, and the world stays the one authority on who sees and changes what. Ink & Switch's Keyhive is working on access control for CRDTs; if it matures, this changes.

Where earlier drafts were wrong

A CRDT saves storage (4 Nov, first redraft).Only against today's whole-text saves. Against small edits it's about even. The case for it is merging, offline, anchors and history.
A comment is detached when its anchors collapse.That misses partial rewrites. The anchor library flags lost characters directly, which gives three states: intact, changed, detached. (reviewer ran)
Agents already know a replace word.They don't. It lived on the archived desk. edit is new kernel code. read
Autosave the whole text every second.That would balloon the in-memory log. ran 4.8 MB for one session against 74 KB as a CRDT.
Comments re-found by quote, then by paragraph ids.Both drift. A CRDT anchor holds the characters themselves. ran 2 of 11 live comments quote a repeated phrase: your two stacked ones.
Combine saves; keep both versions one after the other (3 Nov).Replaced by a CRDT with live cursors, Sable's held-sentence rule for whole-text writers, and an offered Combine.
A side-by-side picker when two people collide.Neighbours shouldn't see a git screen. A mark in the text and both originals in the margin.
The board files documents with no code change.The files module only accepts uploads. Filing documents needs a module change and a sharing rule. read
Store [[id|Title]] behind names.A name in the stored text leaks to people who can't see the thing. Store ids only.
If you approve

Build order

  1. A spike in a private world over copies of the live documents, to settle how it fits Agora before anything else:
    • what the log records at each pause, so fences, undo and the event stream still see typing;
    • each session typing as its own author, and the kernel refusing anyone else's;
    • a refused change becoming that person's suggestion, since their phone can't un-apply it;
    • readers receiving the text without its deleted history;
    • two simulated phones editing offline;
    • the held-sentence rule for whole-text writers.
    Today's Writing editor writes through it, so its “Add them at the end” rescues stop.
  2. Live sync to open pages, with cursors kept out of the log. Ask the group first whether they'd type together (your answer 2).
  3. Comments on anchors, with stacking and detached. Move the 11 existing comments over.
  4. One thing in many places: the board files documents.
  5. Continuous editor: CodeMirror over Loro, the Clean look, the phone selection bar, the painter's n fix.
  6. History view and collision marks, then summaries, Combine and Suggest.
  7. $ references of all three sorts.

The rest of Agora (value kinds that say how they combine, kits that follow their origin, world merges, redact) is a direction to design next, not part of this build.
