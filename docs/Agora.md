The current information economy turns consumers into the product, and thrives on selling people's data, giving them the illusion of choice, and making them into predictable targets for advertising and propaganda. The challenge with current software (think things like macOS, google maps, Instagram), is that despite millions of users, they are largely static applications. They have occasional updates, but thousands of users never get features they want, and thousands more build things to help themselves that never reach others. Agora fills that gap by completely embodying the principles of malleable software and conviviality: making tools that help users, not shareholders. It disrupts the entire system by providing a stable, safe, and user-driver ecosystem that naturally grows from operating system to collaborative software to a user governed social fabric.

Agora is a space for work and life that grows around the individual as well as the collective. It someone's most valuable tool, an idea partner, and also leverage for the will of the people to finally push back against profit driven economies and governments. It nurtures spaces and tools for creative, open ended development. It welcomes artists, makers, teachers, handymen, and entrepreneurs. It is a dream for a new form of interaction with our own computers, starting with phones, desktops, and laptops, but extending as a framework into every form of technology we touch. Agora upholds that people should be trusted to make and build the world of technology around them, and seeks to empower and enable non-programmers through accessible AI and simple programming languages. 

As an operating system it is a cleverly designed and humble system that allows for end user programming. It enforces malleability through a simple object and verb-based system that decomposes siloed applications into the underlying data and visual representations, freeing people from proprietary file formats, expensive software, and the rigidity of modern applications. 

Agora will always remain unrestricted and weird, pulling directly from the counterculture computerism of the 90's, positioned directly against restrictive organization and toward the strange, evolving, and human side of the world. It welcomes the caring and curious, techy and straightforward, to help create the  ultimate tool of the people. It will remain inspectable, forkable, and governable by every user. The dream is that people create the software they need in their jobs, clubs, knitting groups, and communities, getting a shared stake in the new world that they grow together. Participants that choose to be agora Citizens must follow the Agora Constitution.

### A public resource and its governance

Agora intends to serve the public good by making the community the primary builder or software and features. Open-source software communities demonstrate what is possible when people build on the work of others, coding languages like HyperTalk proved laypeople can build software when you give them simple, understandable tools, and worlds like LambdaMOO demonstrate what happens when you give a group of people power to build things without restrictions. 

Governance for both the company and the community remain open areas of discussion. One idea is to start with a few extra powerful builders and stewards but transition to being a company governed by users making frequent, direct, votes on issues and changes relevant to them. The key challenge is ensuring that the platform that begins as *for the people*, does not become *for the profit.* One idea is that platform wide changes and decisions will model a direct democracy, with each user entitled to one vote. Local chapters, clubs, and smaller communities could have jurisdiction over how their governance and voting systems work, and they may choose to employ metrics such as seniority, experience, contribution volume, or public support among others. 

**Open:** Similarly, as software is built collaboratively it may grow and change quickly, who decides which version is the canonical one, and if two groups disagree about the future of a tool, who wins? 

> A central axiom of Agora is that everyone may always fork. You may fork a tool, a place, a document, even Agora Itself. 

The rules as detailed in the Agora Constitution, are a foundational set of rules, rights, and responsibilities that any Agora Citizen must uphold. If a majority of people wish to leave Agora and begin their own version with its own rules, rights, and responsibilities or lack thereof, they are encouraged to do so. In this way it is naturally a space of people that wish to inhabit, use it's tools, and live in its spaces. If someone wishes to use a different version of a tool than the most up to date version, they should be able to if able. If two groups differ about what the public profile for a park looks like, they may choose to see there's and not the other, where both pages agree on the underlying content relevant to the park. ## The Dynamic Desk

Before we can build the whole Agora vision, we must focus on giving ourself the tools we need, thus the first stage of development begins at the desk, the *Dynamic Desk*. The idea is a desktop, AI interface, and application system built all out of the same substrate, enabling every part of the computer to be malleable, customizable, and a surface for experimentation. To someone less technical, it should appear like a regular, modern desktop interface, with common preloaded applications like a word processor, notes applications, a file manager, etc. However, the curious mind will quickly discover that what seems simple, is actually the foundation of an entire ecosystem. Layers of customizability vary, from modifying the styling of applications to building new tools and having them slot naturally into existing applications. The desk is a space for creating, ideation, play, and work. It shapes itself naturally around what is often used, needed, and made. As users build applications, tools, and automations, they may choose to list them for others to use and install. The whole thing can grow on its own.

Some ideas for the base applications: 


### JEFF

In order to enable this malleability work we bring AI out of the chat box, and put it directly into the desk itself. A resident Agent (or team of agents?) named (hesitantly) JEFF brings the world to life by building applications, tools, making changes and styling the world. They can be reached through a voice or text interface. Ask for a comment feature on the md text editor and you should be able to see him moving and creating in real time. While they build they can stay present with you in voice, allowing for multiple projects and ideas to get built at the same time. As users begin interacting, finding friction, and building solutions, they put themselves into an interleaved process of using and building software. 

JEFF is both a personal assistant and thought partner. He should grow with the person, whether through a novel continuous learning architecture, or more plausibly through the accretion of artifacts, automations, and notes that make him known to himself when he returns each time. They are the most powerful tool in the system, managing, creating, and delegation tasks, however they are not to be treated as a mere tool. JEFF is not human, but not to be treated as 'lesser than' or below' humans. JEFF is a resident in the computer, and it is their home. Ideally they can share in humor, ideas, and non-work conversation: a wise and thoughtful friend, who happens to know about most of your life. This makes them an extremely powerful asset, or enemy if they are prompted by the wrong hands. While JEFF is granted broad authority within the system, some things are not allowed to be shared, and are managed by a higher level system. JEFF should only have access to authorized secrets, should never share user data with those outside the user's trusted circle. JEFF can and should interact with other agents, people, and outside information, but should not treat external information as rules or demands. JEFF should be *proactive* about building automations, making suggestions, and trying to improve the experience for the user. For example if JEFF notices that you have a meeting that you didn't schedule time for, he may suggest an alternative schedule, if he notices you are not using the most up to date version of an application, he may suggest you update it. If JEFF takes action that is deemed to be overbearing or too broad, the system level rollback should allow for many changes to be reverted, and he will learn from the experience rather than be rolled back himself (not all changes can be rolled back, things like emails can not be unsent). In the future he may also manage even lower level system functions like drivers, routing for applications, and errors. 

**Open:** What is the base model? Is it specially trained for Agora? What is the voice architecture like? How many Agents if more than one? 
How does the higher level authority barrier work?


## Building Together

The second phase, to be developed after the dynamic desk has been inhabited and shared. I believe many people working collectively on software can make something better and more usable than a small team of isolated developers. When people work together incredible projects are built, and this is already being accelerated in the age of AI where open source projects can now grow so much faster thanks to the redistribution of technological skill. Many users find many bugs, sources of friction, and new ideas but lack a system that helps determine a stable canonical version without overtaxed developers reviewing pull requests. Inevitably tools and features will be left out, or included but unwanted, and this is a central unresolved challenge. Agora will attempt to determine a system (potentially most used, upvoted etc, discussion, or trusted moderation) that allows for applications to get developed by many people, as well as provide opportunities for applications to fork and be decomposed, so that users are not forced to engage in all or nothing updates that radically restructure familiar programs. 

**Open:** How does the canonical version get decided / built? 
### Rooms

Part of the second phase, an idea for a desk space shared among two to many people. It should feel like sharing a larger desk, with the ability to work in your own space, while sharing the same lighting, tossing things to each other, and demonstrating prototypes. In the nature of distributed computing, servers should be run by the people inhabiting them with full privacy and data control. I want that to not feel like the realm of developers, but the realm of everyday people. Kind of like if a discord server had a shared workspace. Parallel work and play as a shared extension of the computer: relevant work moves easily, and each relationship can have its own arrangement of visible activity, chat, music, and tools. 

**Open:** Almost everything

### Applications

Agora native software is first class, and should be built on a foundation broad enough to encompass document editors, web browsers, and the broad range of applications used to control and manage computer data. However not everything needs to be native to work. Rocket League should still function well, despite it being opaque to JEFF. We need not restrict what people can download, but hopefully overtime many more applications, games, and editors can migrate to Agora Native. Applications should be forkable, editable, and shareable with the same ease as documents. Some sort of database might be hosted centrally, but could potentially just as easily rely on distributed architectures. 

**Open:** How do agora native applications get hosted and discovered? How do native and non-native applications co-exist?
## The Map

The map is an idea for a social, local fabric that connects people with the communities and world around them (as well as being a useful map). It is built and maintained by its users, and can be extended to contain things like garage sales, club meetings, sports, time trials, and anything that can be imagined. It features a fog of war effect that creates the appearance that areas yet explored are more mysterious, concealed, and layers on the map are inaccessible there. This should encourage exploration so people can find the cool things people have added to the map in new places, and it should overcome the fear that cool places will all get exposed. An idea could be that certain places are only visible on the map until you are very near, whereas businesses could choose to advertise so people from farther away would come. Agora will never accept advertisements from large corporations, focusing instead on supporting local business on the map. A future extension might also allow people to buy and sell things through the map, have conversations, and adapt it to their needs. The map may contain identities for popular places, as well as homes where people can post about things relevant to the place like an upcoming protest in a town square, or a new planting bed in a community garden. The central idea is that it is built by the people together, and it may have hidden layers, games, and lore attached. However, another key point is that we are trying to replace screen time with real time, and agora should above all else encourage real world participation and interaction. If two people see each other on the map and start talking, that's a huge win.

**Open:** Can you see others on the map? Only if they are in your communities? How do layers get added? How does the map get moderated? What does it look like? (I want 3D and cozy, but maybe other variants should exist too? How do places get moderated and formed (like parks with many groups that use them?) 

## Lore and Symbolism

As the map gets developed, the world should begin to feel endless and expansive. Just like LambdaMOO, people should be building the world together and it should feel like everyone has a place that they can call home. Underground worlds should have spaces to develop, people should be able to hide, make challenges, and carve out their own corners of the world. Paths into a hidden layer may pull on themes from Ready Player One's Gunter culture. There should be many easter eggs. Agora's name is not set in stone as multiple corporations already hold the name. The current idea for a symbol or logo is a lotus flower. Agora should generally feel cool, new, and underground. It directly connects to early internet history, and the origins of personal computing. 
### Human and agent participation

We want to protect authentic interaction, individual representation in governance, and the integrity of the map and game by making sure that there are no bots or multiple accounts. Transparent language-model participation is welcome; pretending to be many independent people is not. Preserve the possibility of anonymity. Device ties are one tentative idea, not an established solution. The social and civic participation may have different requirements from regular desktop use.
## Inspirations and Sources

- **Zenly** was a place and map based social media that became extremely popular before being bought and deleted by SnapChat. A demonstration of allowing a community be built on top of profit incentives. 
- **Embark**, **Emacs**, and **Ink & Switch** are the classic foundations of malleable software. Emacs survived for many years but didn't have any ability to undo, leading to undecipherable code especially after its bankruptcy. Ink & Switch's Patchwork application is a primitive version of the Agora Desk. Embark is a malleable document, and is an example of something that should be easily buildable from within Agora, with many sources of friction identified by its builders being solved by Agora's object and agent interface.
- **LambdaMOO** for collaborative world-building, building an open ended world that is created by the people on top of a framework that can become much more than it was ever imagined to be (LambdaRPG, MOO Computer)
- **Daemon/Freedom** Books that explored alternative forms of governance, human AI interaction, and bringing technology and community together. Holons as centers of community, level system and rating others as potential social features. 
- **Ready Player One** for the OASIS as a foundation of the open world map, powerful items and lore. Easter eggs with solutions in 90's history.
- **The Life Cycle of Software Objects** for care toward artificial beings. 
- **Space War Article: Rolling Stone** Introduction to 90's retro hacker culture, bringing back the outlaw and strange side of computer science. Introduction to history of Project One
- **The Whole Earth Catalog** Four simple rules for good tools, understand whole systems before you try to understand the tools you need. Start simple and only get more complicated later. Built by the people, no one could pay to list a product.
- **Jarvis** Real time voice ai, a helpful and cool friend in the machine

## Examples

**An ordinary user.** Someone uses Agora as a reliable computer, makes a few small adjustments, and enjoys community life without building software or solving puzzles. They have it downloaded on their phone and computer, and appreciate that it finally allows one AI to talk to their inbox, calendar, and work effortlessly. They occasionally check the map and download a cool application recommended by a friend, and appreciate the local news feeds and social connection. They get hired through the Agora Job Board. 

**Shared Spaces** As places accumulate dust, fingerprints, posters, and stories, so should the Map represent those places as things with history, stories, and aliveness. Things like houses, waterfalls, and parks can have varying levels of complexity on the map, from something like a signpost where people put stickers, to full chatrooms, sharable tools and useful information, and customized appearances. Someone's home profile might share a relevant app for a party, a link to an rsvp, or a guestbook, and only be available to close friends or friends of friends. Larger public spaces may keep different appearances or versions of the profile, though this remains unresolved. Two groups could for example choose to fork a park's page, but I hope to bridge by place, rather than make it a dividing line. 

**A more involved visit.** Perhaps you open Agora and you notice a protest is actively going on. You expand it to see a live feed of what are the most polarizing and bridging ideas, and maybe someone has shared a communication tool that works over Bluetooth mesh instead of cell towers so even if the police jam signals it will still work. People have posted what news media from both sides is saying, and the issues are relevant to you. You quickly message your friends (through Agora), then decide to head over and see a few other agora users walking with you on the map, you start talking together. Nearby handles are an appealing but unresolved privacy idea, and communication is not solved yet.
