# The Agora Constitution

**Unratified statement of direction · revised September 9, 2026.**

Agora should be a place people can trust with their work and reshape without
losing ownership of it. This note keeps the values that motivate the project.
It does not establish a legal entity, a governance schedule, or an immutable
technical constitution. Current license terms are determined by actual license
instruments, not by aspirations in this document.

## The person

The person directs their environment. Useful controls, understandable state,
privacy, recovery and the ability to leave matter more than locking someone
into an implementation. Personal work should not become a price paid for access
to an assistant. Export and self-hosting are important directions to prove in
working software, with honest accounts of dependencies and unavailable resources.

## JEFF

JEFF is a capable collaborator, shaped by the person's intent and experience of
working together. It should be able to develop new capabilities, explain what
it changed, learn from failures, and continue useful work without needless
permission prompts when that work is already authorized.

Responsiveness, continuity, inspectable activity and cancellation are product
goals. They do not prescribe one permanent agent topology, one model provider,
ephemeral workers, or an irreversible memory store. Retaining lessons from a
failed experiment can coexist with correction, deletion and bounded retention.

## A malleable and recoverable environment

The current architecture keeps authoritative native state, credentials and
independent recovery outside the authored Linux workspace and shell. This lets
the environment change substantially while the person can still stop work and
recover a usable view. [[Security]] explains the implemented boundaries and
their limits.

The identity of a document is not its current window. A tag is useful context,
not authority. Several views and contributors may coexist. The project has no
constitutional choice of storage encoding, solver, coordinate arithmetic,
merge algorithm, scheduler or visual language.

## The record and the right to change course

Current native writes enter an authenticated gateway and durable journal.
Replay reconstructs recorded native state without repeating external effects.
That mechanism does not make all files, services, human observations or physical
events reversible. Retention and erasure need explicit policies as those systems
are developed; preserving a lesson does not require retaining every payload.

Openness, auditability and a practical fork/exit path are central to the vision.
Engineering standards can change through ordinary evidence-based development.
Changes affecting people's actual rights deserve their direct consideration;
they need not turn every new facet or interface into constitutional legislation.

## Shared life and stewardship

Agora could grow from a personal workspace into shared rooms and public places.
People should retain meaningful control over participation and collective
decisions. Agents can help people understand choices without quietly acquiring
their authority. Governance, financing, organizational form and transitions of
stewardship remain matters to work out with the people involved. No speculative
calendar, ownership structure, revenue threshold or voting procedure is imposed
on today's software development. See [[Rooms]], [[Democracy]] and [[Ownership]].
