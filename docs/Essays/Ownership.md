# Ownership

**Stewardship vision, not legal or organizational terms.**

A computing environment that holds someone's work should earn trust through
understandable behavior, inspectable machinery and practical ways to leave.
Source availability, interoperable exports and self-hosting are valuable directions
because they reduce dependence on one operator's continuing goodwill.

Openness and community stewardship are goals. This essay does not select a legal
entity, amend a software license, establish contribution terms, prohibit categories
of financing or promise that an organization is legally unsellable. Existing
license instruments govern current distribution. Future legal and organizational
choices need explicit decisions when there is something concrete to govern.

Hosted service, compute, support and other arrangements could fund work without
selling control of private content. The appropriate structure should be judged
by actual incentives, operating needs, accountability and the user's ability to
retain their work. No speculative revenue percentage or company hierarchy should
become a requirement for writing a useful local application.

The technical task today is tangible: make recovery, export and understandable
dependencies work. A source archive is useful; a source archive that cannot
reconstruct the promised content should not be called a complete exit path.
