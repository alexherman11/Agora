# Facets in everyday use

**Examples for testing the model.**

A paper can keep its title and source while being read in a browser, cited in a
memo and shown as a card on the desk. Its reading position is personal; a shared
annotation may have an explicit audience. A live source and a pinned quotation
can coexist without pretending their update behavior is the same.

A document can gain review comments, a publication state or a template preference
without becoming a new rigid class. Tags help choose a view or style, while schemas
and installed capabilities determine what actions actually work. A generic
inspector can make a new field useful before a custom interface exists.

Music has a recording, a playlist occurrence and a playback activity. A device
has an identity and an observed availability. A job's desired state is not proof
that it is running. These distinctions help ordinary use; they do not require
every transient value to become a durable entity or every activity a reconciler.

The useful test is whether the person can keep doing their work while a view or
capability changes. Try editing text while rearranging its window, exporting an
annotation without private history, and opening two views of one subject. The
current implementation and remaining gaps are in [[Implementation Status]] and
[[Objects and Facets]].
