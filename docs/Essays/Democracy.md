# Democracy

**A direction for shared life, not a governance implementation.**

Agora could eventually support communities as well as individual workspaces.
People should be able to understand collective choices, participate meaningfully,
and leave without losing control of their work. An assistant can summarize a
discussion, expose disagreement or help someone formulate a proposal. Those
abilities do not silently make the assistant the person's representative.

Decision processes should fit the people and the stakes. Small groups may need
very little formal governance; larger shared infrastructure may need accountable
stewardship, appeal and explicit delegation. Elections, consensus, sortition and
other processes are options to investigate, not predetermined platform features.

Attention and participation are limited resources. A useful civic interface could
help people see what affects them without demanding constant engagement. It
should not assume one heat score can settle relevance, memory and legitimacy.

There is no fixed founder sunset calendar, revenue threshold, foundation structure
or voting mechanism in the present engineering plan. Those choices require actual
participants and circumstances. [[Constitution]] keeps the values separate from
routine technical evolution; [[Rooms]] explores a nearer shared-work experiment.
